Benchmarks

The numbers, with their caveats.

Two kinds of numbers live here. One user's month: the author's own 30 days of Claude Code, aggregated (408 sessions, 71,511 requests; no project names, no prompts). The parts agento is built from: how far the judges can be trusted and how fast the local student model is.

None of it is a measured saving, and none of it generalises to you. The public benchmark (cost per resolved task against always-Opus, opusplan and rule routers) is on the roadmap. Run npx agento-cc audit to see your own version of the first half.

Aggregates from the author's own 30 days of Claude Code use (one subscription user, API-equivalent prices). Not a benchmark across users.

04 Why agento never switches mid-task

Steps until a mid-task model switch pays off

Switching rewrites the whole cached prefix for the new model (the penalty). It pays off only after enough cheaper steps. Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 read the cache at the same price, so the gain per step is small. Simulated at two cached-prefix sizes.

Assumes the cheaper model does the task in as many steps as the original, which it often does not. That is why the model is picked at a clean point, where there is no cache to lose, and why work inside a task moves to subagents instead. The worked example →

05 What ran vs what was needed

54%of the author's Opus/Fable tasks (303 of 560) would have been fine on Sonnet or Haiku, according to an Opus judge.

Judge opinion on finished tasks, not a replay. Opus read each finished task (prompt, diff, a compressed trajectory) and named the cheapest model it thinks would have done as well. Nobody re-ran these tasks on Sonnet or Haiku. The next section shows how often such a judge is wrong.

06 Judges compared

Under-routing is the number that matters.

A router can be wrong in two directions. Over-routing, picking a more expensive model than needed, only loses savings: you pay what you paid before. Under-routing, picking a cheaper model than needed, costs quality: a broken change, a retry, a long debugging loop that eats more than the saving.

So accuracy alone is misleading. A trained model ships only if its under-routing on held-out tasks is at most 5% at the chosen confidence threshold. The judges below are far from that, which is why their labels are calibrated against hand labels and replays before training, and never used as-is.

under = judge picked a cheaper model than the reference (quality risk); over = more expensive (missed savings).

“Opus and Qwen” compares the two judges with each other on the same tasks; neither is ground truth. The author's labels are hand labels of his own past tasks (34 for Opus).

* TwinRouterBench SWE-bench step prefixes; reference labels there are weak (degradation_search_done).

08 First training run

The student learned something useful, and something it must not do alone.

◆ verdict opus-v1 · 2026-10-05

From the prompt alone the student cannot pick the model safely (no threshold keeps under-routing <= 5%), so it never acts on its own for the tier. Its plan-first (92%) and delegate-explore (76%) heads are usable. Next: decide after the first steps, when trajectory signals exist.
